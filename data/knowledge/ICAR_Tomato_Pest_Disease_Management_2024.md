# ICAR Agricultural Advisory Bulletin: Solanaceous Crop Disorders (2024 Edition)
**Publisher**: Indian Council of Agricultural Research (ICAR) & National Institute of Plant Health Management (NIPHM)
**Crop Focus**: Tomato (*Solanum lycopersicum*), Chilli (*Capsicum annuum*)
**Reference Code**: ICAR-SOL-2024-V4

---

## Section 1: Viral Diseases - Tomato Yellow Leaf Curl Virus (TYLCV)

### 1.1 Etiology and Symptoms
Tomato Yellow Leaf Curl Virus (TYLCV) is a begomovirus (family *Geminiviridae*) transmitted persistently by the sweetpotato whitefly (*Bemisia tabaci*). 
Characteristic foliar symptoms include:
- Severe upward curling and cupping of leaflet margins.
- Interveinal chlorosis and yellowing along leaf margins of young shoots.
- Reduction in leaflet size, giving a stunted "bonsai" or bushy canopy appearance.
- Abscission of flowers when infection occurs prior to fruit set, leading to yield loss up to 100%.

### 1.2 Meteorological and Vector Dynamics
*Bemisia tabaci* vectors exhibit rapid generation cycles and reproductive bursts during hot, arid conditions where ambient daily temperatures exceed 32°C to 36°C and relative humidity drops below 40%. Conversely, heavy precipitation and relative humidity above 75% mechanically disrupt adult whitefly flight and encourage entomopathogenic fungal mortality.

### 1.3 Integrated Pest & Disease Management (IPM) Recommendations
1. **Nursery Protection**: Raise seedlings under 40-50 mesh insect-proof nylon netting for at least 25 to 30 days post-germination.
2. **Physical Vector Interception**: Erect yellow sticky traps (15-20 traps/acre) placed 15 cm above the crop canopy for continuous population monitoring.
3. **Biological Control**: Conserve or release natural predators such as *Chrysoperla carnea* (green lacewing) and *Encarsia formosa* parasitoid wasps.
4. **Cultural Management**: Maintain barrier crops of 2-3 rows of maize, sorghum, or pearl millet around tomato plots to physically impede whitefly migration. Avoid planting adjacent to old cucurbit or cotton fields.

## Section 2: Abiotic Disorders - Physiological Leaf Roll

### 2.1 Symptoms and Differentiation
Physiological leaf roll in tomato is an abiotic response to transpirational stress, excessive soil moisture fluctuation, or high ambient solar radiation. 
Key differentiation from TYLCV:
- Upward rolling typically begins on older, lower leaves and progresses upward (in contrast to TYLCV, which manifests earliest on the newest apical foliage).
- Foliage remains firm, leathery, and dark green without interveinal yellowing or chlorosis.
- Fruit set is generally unaffected if floral irrigation is maintained.

### 2.2 Mitigation Next Steps
- Implement drip irrigation with scheduled frequency to stabilize root-zone soil moisture.
- Apply light organic mulching (paddy straw or sugarcane bagasse) at 5 tonnes/ha to suppress soil temperature and preserve moisture.
